# Competitor research

Researched 2026-11-05. Facts come from each product's own pages unless noted. Products change, so re-check before relying on details.

## Summary

| Product | What it does well | Gap Switchback targets |
|---|---|---|
| Brilliant | Interactive, visual lessons; AI tutor that guides instead of answering | CS stops at intro level; puzzles rather than building; no review schedule found |
| roadmap.sh | A huge, popular map of what to learn | Doesn't teach; progress is self-reported |
| Math Academy | Adaptive mastery and prerequisite-aware spaced review | Math only |
| Engram (developer learning engine) | Spaced review and first-principles curricula for developers | Closest concept found; worth watching |

## Brilliant (brilliant.org)

- Short, visual, interactive lessons ("learn by doing"); learners write and run Python in the browser.
- **Koji**, an AI tutor that "asks guiding questions, sketches on your screen, and helps you work through each step. He gets you to the answer without giving it to you."
- Assessments: skill checks at the end of every lesson; personalized practice from your history; unit checks that **interleave** prerequisite topics; a skill graph of prerequisites with adaptive questions that find missing foundations; mock tests rolling out November 2026.
- CS coverage tops out around intro programming, Big-O, basic data structures (linked lists, binary search trees) and graph search. Paths: Python, Computer Science, Technology, AI, Data. 40+ courses in total.
- "Coding Skills Framework": Foundations of CS (7 big ideas, 42 objectives, 196 skills) and Coding with AI (7 big ideas, 37 objectives, 106 skills).
- No spaced-repetition scheduling is mentioned anywhere.
- Reviews: one calls the CS courses "more akin to brain teasers and interview preparation exercises rather than comprehensive lessons"; another says introductions are "often brief and sometimes shallow."
- Price: Premium listed at about $30/month (unclear whether that's the monthly or annual rate); family plan available.

## roadmap.sh

- 97 community-made roadmaps: 31 role-based (Backend, AI Engineer, DevOps…), 61 skill-based (Computer Science, Data Structures & Algorithms, System Design…), 5 best-practice guides.
- Very popular: 371K GitHub stars and 3.2M registered users, per the site.
- The Computer Science roadmap is a "curriculum with free resources for a self-taught developer." Topics mostly point to other people's articles and videos.
- Learners mark their own progress (done, in progress, skip) with no verification. *(From general knowledge; the fetched pages didn't show this.)*
- 2026 additions: an AI Tutor on every roadmap (launched February 2026); AI-generated courses, guides, roadmaps and quizzes; a personal AI coach; small "lesson packs" with projects; a video course on LLMs; certificates.
- Pricing: a free tier with one-time AI allowances; Pro about $8.33/month billed yearly; Teams $10 per seat per month.

## Math Academy (mathacademy.com)

- A knowledge graph of thousands of interlinked math topics, from 4th grade to university level, including math for machine learning.
- Mastery learning; an adaptive diagnostic that finds missing foundations; and **FIRe** (Fractional Implicit Repetition): reviewing an advanced topic gives partial credit to the simpler topics it uses, and failing one penalizes the topics it depends on.
- Math only. I found no CS courses.
- The closest existing model to Switchback's engine.

## Engram (developer learning engine)

- Reported launching July 2026: a "learning engine" for developers combining FSRS spaced repetition, first-principles curricula and free-recall checks. Distributed as a Claude Code plugin; about 775 GitHub stars reported.
- At least three other learning apps also use the Engram name.
- The closest concept found. Worth studying, not a reason to stop.

## Others (general knowledge, not re-checked this session)

- **Execute Program:** spaced-repetition courses on programming topics (TypeScript, SQL, regular expressions).
- **Boot.dev:** gamified backend-development courses with in-browser coding.
- **CodeCrafters:** "build your own X" challenges (Redis, Git, SQLite…) checked by tests.
- **Hyperskill (JetBrains Academy):** a knowledge map plus projects.
- **Launch School:** a slow, mastery-based programming curriculum with assessments.
- **Exercism:** practice problems with mentoring.
- **OSSU and Teach Yourself CS:** free curricula built from existing courses and books.
- **Khan Academy (Khanmigo):** an AI tutor that guides rather than answers.
