<Intro>

A vector is a list of numbers that belong together: a point's coordinates, a day's measurements, a word turned into numbers. A matrix is a grid of them. Nearly everything a machine-learning model does is adding, scaling and multiplying these, and in this topic you'll do each one with plain Python lists and loops, so that when NumPy does it for you later you know exactly what it did.

The main resource is the free textbook *OpenStax Precalculus 2e*: **section 8.8, Vectors** (the first half, up to the dot product) and **section 9.5, Matrices and Matrix Operations**. About 25 minutes. If you'd rather watch, the first four chapters of 3Blue1Brown's *Essence of linear algebra* (linked below the lesson) show the same ideas as pictures, and chapter 9 does the dot product. Watch for:

- **Adding vectors** is adding matching entries; **scaling** multiplies every entry by the same number.
- **The dot product** multiplies matching entries and adds the results: one number, not a vector. A vector's **length** is the square root of its dot product with itself.
- **A matrix's shape** is rows × columns, and two matrices can be multiplied only when the inner numbers match: a 2×3 times a 3×4 gives a 2×4.
- **Each entry of a product** is the dot product of a row and a column.

Hold on to the shape rule. It's the single most common error in machine-learning code.

</Intro>

<Recap>

- **A vector is a list:** `a = [1, 2, 3]`. **Add** entry by entry with a loop over `range(len(a))`: `[1, 2, 3] + [4, 5, 6]` is `[5, 7, 9]`. **Scale** every entry: `3 * [1, 2, 3]` is `[3, 6, 9]`. (Python's own `+` on lists glues them together, and `3 * a` repeats the list: that's why we loop.)
- **The dot product** is `a[0] * b[0] + a[1] * b[1] + ...`: for `[1, 2, 3]` and `[4, 5, 6]` it's `4 + 10 + 18`, `32`. One number. Perpendicular vectors have a dot product of 0.
- **Length:** the square root of the dot product of a vector with itself: `[3, 4]` has length `(9 + 16) ** 0.5`, `5.0`.
- **A matrix is a list of rows:** `m = [[1, 2], [3, 4]]`; `m[i][j]` is row i, column j. Its **shape** is `(len(m), len(m[0]))`: rows × columns.
- **Matrix × vector:** each entry of the result is the dot product of one row with the vector. `[[1, 2], [3, 4]]` times `[5, 6]` is `[1*5 + 2*6, 3*5 + 4*6]`, `[17, 39]`.
- **Matrix × matrix:** entry `[i][j]` of the product is the dot product of row i of the first and column j of the second. The inner shapes must match (the columns of the first, the rows of the second) and the result takes the outer ones: `2×3` times `3×4` is `2×4`. Three loops: rows, columns, and the shared index.
- **Later, in machine learning:** a *neuron* is one weighted sum of the inputs (a dot product of the inputs with a row of numbers called *weights*), and a *layer* is a whole set of neurons computed at once: a matrix (the weights, one row per neuron) times a vector (the inputs), one dot product per neuron; a data set is a matrix with one row per example; and "shape mismatch" is the error you'll see most, for exactly the reason above.

</Recap>
