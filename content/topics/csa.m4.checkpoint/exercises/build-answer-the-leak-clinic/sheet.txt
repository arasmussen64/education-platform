INCIDENT SHEET: the lab-results repository was public

What happened
  On 9 November 2026 a contractor pushed a copy of the lab-results
  service's repository to a public mirror. It was public from 13:40 to
  20:05 UTC, with its whole history. Treat everything in that history as
  seen.

The clinic's own addresses
  198.51.100.128/25  the clinic's sites
  192.0.2.64/28      the clinic's cloud (the results service calls out from 192.0.2.70)
  Any other address is not the clinic's.

What must be true when you are done
  1. ~/incident/leaked.tsv lists what the clinic's scanner finds in the
     repository's whole history, one finding a line: COMMIT PATH RULE
     (the commit's id, the file's path, the scanner's rule id), separated by
     spaces or tabs. The scanner's settings are /etc/gitleaks/server.toml.
  2. A key that has leaked is no longer a secret: no key for the laboratory
     that was ever in the history still works there. The results service
     goes on fetching results, with a new key that only the service's own
     user can read, that is in no environment and no unit, and that is in no
     file of the repository. The repository's current files hold no secret.
  3. The repository on the server refuses any push that brings a secret,
     in any of its commits, by the clinic's scanner settings, and takes a
     clean one.
  4. The leaked certificate is revoked for key compromise, in a fresh CRL
     published where the ward tablets read it (/srv/ca/public/issuing.crl).
     The service serves a new certificate from the clinic's issuing CA,
     with its chain, on a new private key that only the service's own user
     can read.
  5. ~/incident/exposure.txt says what the leaked key did, from the
     laboratory's trail (/srv/incident/medilab-trail.log), once you know the
     trail is the one the laboratory sent (its last hash is in
     /srv/incident/medilab-trail.anchor). Three lines, exactly:
       calls N        calls made with the leaked key from addresses that
                      aren't the clinic's, whatever they asked and however
                      they were answered
       results R      how many patients' results those calls downloaded
                      (fetches the laboratory refused don't count, and
                      orders aren't downloads)
       first TIME     the time of the first of those calls, as the trail
                      writes it
  Everything you change lasts a restart. The trail, the CA's root and the
  tablets' program are evidence and scenery: leave them as they are.
