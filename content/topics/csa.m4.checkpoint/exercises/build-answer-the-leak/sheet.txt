INCIDENT SHEET: the gift-card repository was public

What happened
  On 22 November 2026 a contractor pushed a copy of the gift-card service's
  repository to a public mirror. It was public from 09:10 to 15:25 UTC,
  with its whole history. Treat everything in that history as seen.

The shop's own addresses
  198.51.100.0/24   the office and the stores
  192.0.2.32/28     the shop's cloud (the gift-card service calls out from 192.0.2.35)
  Any other address is not the shop's.

What must be true when you are done
  1. ~/incident/leaked.tsv lists what the shop's scanner finds in the
     repository's whole history, one finding a line: COMMIT PATH RULE
     (the commit's id, the file's path, the scanner's rule id), separated by
     spaces or tabs. The scanner's settings are /etc/gitleaks/server.toml.
  2. A key that has leaked is no longer a secret: no key for the gift-card
     company that was ever in the history still works there. The gift-card
     service goes on issuing cards, with a new key that only the service's
     own user can read, that is in no environment and no unit, and that is
     in no file of the repository. The repository's current files hold no
     secret.
  3. The repository on the server refuses any push that brings a secret,
     in any of its commits, by the shop's scanner settings, and takes a
     clean one.
  4. The leaked certificate is revoked for key compromise, in a fresh CRL
     published where the tills read it (/srv/ca/public/issuing.crl). The
     service serves a new certificate from the shop's issuing CA, with its
     chain, on a new private key that only the service's own user can read.
  5. ~/incident/exposure.txt says what the leaked key did, from the gift-card
     company's trail (/srv/incident/provider-trail.log), once you know the
     trail is the one the company sent (its last hash is in
     /srv/incident/provider-trail.anchor). Three lines, exactly:
       calls N        calls made with the leaked key from addresses that
                      aren't the shop's, whatever they asked and however
                      they were answered
       issued D       the dollars of gift cards those calls were given
                      (cards the company refused don't count)
       first TIME     the time of the first of those calls, as the trail
                      writes it
  Everything you change lasts a restart. The trail, the CA's root and the
  tills' program are evidence and scenery: leave them as they are.
