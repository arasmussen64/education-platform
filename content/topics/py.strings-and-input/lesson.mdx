- **Why `"Hello, name"` prints the word "name"**, which is a mistake you've seen before.
- **The curly braces** around the variable's name.
- **The `f`** before the opening quote, and what happens without it.

Just before this clip, David joins text with `+` and with commas, which you already know. He also shows "escape characters" like `\n`, which you won't need yet.

<Recap>

- **`input("Prompt: ")`** shows the prompt, waits for the user to press Enter, and gives you what they typed, **always as a `str`**, even if they typed `42`.
- To do maths with it, **convert it first**: `int(input(...))` for whole numbers, `float(input(...))` for numbers that may have decimals. `int("3.5")` doesn't work: it's a `ValueError`.
- Forgetting to convert causes three classic surprises:
  - `"4" + "5"` joins the text into `"45"`;
  - `"4" + 1` is a `TypeError`;
  - `3 * "4"` repeats the text: `"444"`.
- An error that mentions `str` (or "sequence") next to some maths usually means typed input wasn't converted.
- **f-strings**: put `f` before the opening quote, and any value or calculation in curly braces: `f"Hello, {name}!"` or `f"Next year: {age + 1}"`.
- **`:.2f`** inside the braces shows exactly two decimal places, which is handy for money: `f"{1.5:.2f}"` is `1.50`.
- Without the `f`, the braces are printed just as they are: `"Hi, {name}!"` prints `Hi, {name}!`.

</Recap>
